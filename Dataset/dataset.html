02 / Research Motivation

Why Do We Need Our Own Dataset?

Existing public datasets provide valuable training resources. However, models developed exclusively on external datasets may not adequately represent the conditions of real-world transportation in Bangladesh.

01

Domain Adaptation

Differences in camera placement, lighting, recording environments and driver characteristics can affect model performance. Real-world recordings help us investigate these differences.

02

Behavioral Diversity

Natural driving behavior cannot be fully represented through controlled or publicly available recordings. Real-world observations help broaden our understanding of driver behavior.

03

Reliable Evaluation

Independent real-world recordings are necessary to investigate whether the developed models can perform beyond their original training datasets.

03 / Dataset Methodology

How Are We Building the Dataset?

The collected recordings will pass through a structured processing pipeline before being integrated into model development.

STEP 01

Collection

Gather real-world driver recordings and document recording conditions and available metadata.

STEP 02

Preprocessing

Extract relevant video frames, standardize input dimensions and inspect recording quality.

STEP 03

Annotation

Identify observable events and label facial, behavioral and temporal observations.

STEP 04

Augmentation

Apply controlled transformations to training samples to increase data diversity.

STEP 05

Integration

Organize the resulting data for model training, validation and independent evaluation.

Data integrity: Original recordings will be preserved. Augmented samples will be generated only after the dataset partitions are established, preventing augmented versions of training samples from leaking into independent evaluation data.
04 / Multimodal Architecture

What Are We Extracting?

G.U.A.R.D.I.A.N. combines multiple information sources rather than relying on facial expressions alone. Each modality contributes different evidence to the overall driver-risk assessment.

Facial Features

Facial landmarks and temporal facial observations are used to identify indicators associated with drowsiness and driver attention.

Features:
Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR)
Mouth Aspect Ratio (MAR)
Head orientation
Eye closure duration

Body Behavior

Pose estimation and movement analysis help identify unusual posture and changes in body behavior that may indicate driving risks.

Features:
Shoulder alignment
Torso orientation
Arm movement
Postural changes

Vehicle Telemetry

GPS and available sensor information provide contextual evidence about vehicle movement and driving conditions.

Features:
GPS speed
Vehicle location
Acceleration (if available)
Speed-limit comparison

07 / Data Structuring & Database Architecture

How Will Raw Recordings Become Model-Ready Data?

The dataset must be more than a collection of video files. Each usable observation needs a traceable source, timestamp, extracted features and clearly defined annotations. The following workflow and schema describe the proposed structure for organizing that information.

PROCESS 02

PROCESS 03

Feature Extraction

Calculate validated facial and body measurements; align telemetry where available.

PROCESS 04

Recording traceability

Each session is linked to an anonymized driver code and its original source recording. This supports session-level separation and traceability without exposing unnecessary identity information.

Separate feature modalities

Facial and body measurements are stored independently and linked to their source frame. This avoids mixing distinct measurement types into one ambiguous record.

Time-based annotations

Labels can cover a time interval rather than a single frame. The annotation record therefore stores a session reference and start/end timestamps.

Augmentation lineage

Every generated sample should retain its source reference and transformation details, making it possible to identify derived data and audit the training pipeline.

Design and evaluation safeguards: Split original recordings by driver or recording session before augmentation. Apply augmentation only to the training partition; keep validation and test recordings independent. Five recordings are an initial pipeline baseline, not sufficient evidence of generalization across drivers. GPS and other telemetry should be stored as separately timestamped records if their sampling rate differs from the video frame rate.
06 / Expected Outcome

From Raw Data to Driver Intelligence

The objective is to develop a structured and increasingly diverse dataset that supports the training of reliable multimodal driver-monitoring models. Through real-world collection, feature extraction, annotation and controlled augmentation, G.U.A.R.D.I.A.N. aims to improve the relevance of its training data while preserving independent evaluation for meaningful performance assessment.

Our focus is not simply generating more data. It is generating more useful, representative and scientifically defensible training data.
